# ☀️ SUN

**Type:** G-type main-sequence star (G2 V)
**Age:** ~4.6 billion years
**Diameter:** ~1.39 million km
**Mass:** ~1.989 × 10³⁰ kg
**Surface temperature:** ~5,500 °C
**Core temperature:** ~15 million °C
**Distance from Earth:** ~149.6 million km (1 AU)

### Description

The Sun is the star at the center of our Solar System and contains about **99.8% of the Solar System's total mass**. Its gravity keeps the planets, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, and other objects in orbit.

The Sun is composed primarily of **hydrogen and helium**.

Deep inside its core, enormous pressure and temperature allow hydrogen nuclei to fuse together, producing helium. This process is called **nuclear fusion** and releases enormous amounts of energy.

That energy eventually reaches the Sun's surface and is emitted as light and other electromagnetic radiation.

### Structure

The Sun is not a solid object. Its major layers include:

* Core
* Radiative zone
* Convection zone
* Photosphere
* Chromosphere
* Corona

The visible "surface" is the **photosphere**.

The outermost atmosphere, the **corona**, is extremely hot and extends far into space.

### Interesting facts

* Sunlight takes about **8 minutes 20 seconds** to reach Earth.
* The Sun rotates faster at its equator than near its poles.
* Solar activity produces **solar flares and coronal mass ejections**.
* The Sun will eventually become a **red giant** before ending its life as a white dwarf.

# ☿ MERCURY

**Type:** Terrestrial planet
**Distance from Sun:** ~57.9 million km
**Diameter:** ~4,879 km
**Year:** ~88 Earth days
**Day:** ~58.6 Earth days
**Moons:** 0
**Atmosphere:** Extremely thin exosphere

### Description

Mercury is the **smallest planet** in the Solar System and the closest planet to the Sun.

Its surface is heavily covered with impact craters, making it resemble Earth's Moon.

Because Mercury has almost no substantial atmosphere, it cannot efficiently distribute heat around the planet.

As a result, temperatures vary enormously between the day and night sides.

### Surface

Mercury has:

* enormous craters
* rocky plains
* cliffs
* impact basins
* permanently shadowed polar regions

One of its most famous structures is the **Caloris Basin**, a gigantic impact feature.

### Interesting facts

* Mercury has the shortest year of all planets.
* Despite being closest to the Sun, Mercury is **not the hottest planet**.
* Ice exists inside permanently shadowed craters near its poles.
* Mercury has a surprisingly large iron core.

# ♀ VENUS

**Type:** Terrestrial planet
**Distance from Sun:** ~108.2 million km
**Diameter:** ~12,104 km
**Year:** ~224.7 Earth days
**Day:** ~243 Earth days
**Moons:** 0
**Atmosphere:** ~96.5% carbon dioxide

### Description

Venus is Earth's neighboring planet and is approximately similar in size to Earth.

But its environment is radically different.

Venus has an extremely dense atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide. This creates an intense **runaway greenhouse effect**, making Venus the hottest planet in the Solar System.

### Surface

The surface contains:

* volcanic plains
* mountains
* enormous volcanoes
* lava flows
* impact craters

The surface pressure is approximately **90 times Earth's sea-level atmospheric pressure**.

### Interesting facts

* Venus is hotter than Mercury despite being farther from the Sun.
* Venus rotates **backward** compared with most planets.
* One Venusian rotation takes longer than its year.
* Its clouds contain sulfuric acid droplets.
* Venus may have experienced enormous volcanic activity relatively recently.

# 🌍 EARTH

**Type:** Terrestrial planet
**Distance from Sun:** ~149.6 million km
**Diameter:** ~12,742 km
**Year:** 365.25 days
**Day:** ~24 hours
**Moons:** 1
**Atmosphere:** ~78% nitrogen, ~21% oxygen

### Description

Earth is the only planet currently known to support **life**.

It contains large quantities of liquid water on its surface and has an atmosphere that provides suitable conditions for biological activity.

Earth's magnetic field helps protect the atmosphere and surface from charged particles emitted by the Sun.

### Surface

Earth consists of:

* oceans
* continents
* mountains
* deserts
* forests
* glaciers
* rivers
* volcanoes

Approximately **71% of Earth's surface is covered by water**.

### Moon

Earth has one natural satellite: **the Moon**.

The Moon influences Earth's tides and helps stabilize Earth's axial orientation.

### Interesting facts

* Earth is the densest planet in the Solar System.
* It has active plate tectonics.
* Its atmosphere protects life from much harmful solar and cosmic radiation.
* Earth is currently the only world known to have biological ecosystems.

# ♂ MARS

**Type:** Terrestrial planet
**Distance from Sun:** ~227.9 million km
**Diameter:** ~6,779 km
**Year:** ~687 Earth days
**Day:** ~24.6 hours
**Moons:** 2
**Atmosphere:** Mostly carbon dioxide

### Description

Mars is commonly called the **Red Planet** because iron minerals in its surface soil have oxidized, producing a reddish appearance.

Mars is much smaller than Earth but has a day length surprisingly similar to Earth's.

### Surface

Mars contains some of the most dramatic geological structures known in the Solar System.

These include:

**Olympus Mons**

The largest known volcano in the Solar System.

**Valles Marineris**

A gigantic canyon system stretching thousands of kilometers across the Martian surface.

Mars also contains polar ice caps made primarily of water ice and seasonal carbon dioxide ice.

### Moons

Mars has two small moons:

* Phobos
* Deimos

### Interesting facts

* Mars once had flowing surface water.
* Ancient river valleys and lakebeds remain visible.
* Scientists search Mars for evidence of ancient microbial habitability.
* Mars is a major target for future human exploration.

# ♃ JUPITER

**Type:** Gas giant
**Distance from Sun:** ~778.5 million km
**Diameter:** ~139,820 km
**Year:** ~11.86 Earth years
**Day:** ~9.9 hours
**Moons:** 95+ known
**Atmosphere:** Mostly hydrogen and helium

### Description

Jupiter is the **largest planet in the Solar System**.

It is so massive that it contains more than twice the mass of all the other planets combined.

Jupiter does not have a solid surface that a spacecraft could land on like Earth or Mars.

Instead, its atmosphere becomes progressively denser and transitions into exotic high-pressure states deeper inside.

### Great Red Spot

Jupiter's most famous feature is the **Great Red Spot**, a gigantic storm larger than Earth.

It has existed for centuries, although its size and appearance have changed over time.

### Magnetic field

Jupiter has an enormous magnetic field.

Its magnetosphere is the largest planetary magnetic environment in the Solar System.

### Interesting facts

* Jupiter rotates extremely quickly.
* It produces powerful auroras.
* Its moon system includes fascinating worlds such as Europa, Io, Ganymede, and Callisto.
* Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System.

# ♄ SATURN

**Type:** Gas giant
**Distance from Sun:** ~1.43 billion km
**Diameter:** ~116,460 km
**Year:** ~29.45 Earth years
**Day:** ~10.7 hours
**Moons:** 140+ known
**Atmosphere:** Mostly hydrogen and helium

### Description

Saturn is the **second-largest planet** and is famous for its spectacular ring system.

Saturn's rings are not a solid structure. They consist primarily of enormous numbers of pieces of **water ice and rocky material**, ranging from tiny particles to large chunks.

### Ring system

The major rings are divided into many sections and gaps.

The rings are extremely wide but comparatively thin.

### Moons

Saturn has a remarkable collection of moons.

The most famous include:

* Titan
* Enceladus
* Rhea
* Iapetus
* Mimas

**Titan** has a thick atmosphere and liquid methane/ethane lakes.

**Enceladus** has an underground ocean and shoots water-rich plumes into space.

### Interesting facts

* Saturn's average density is lower than water.
* Its rings are relatively young on astronomical timescales.
* Saturn has enormous atmospheric storms.
* Its north pole contains a strange persistent **hexagonal jet-stream pattern**.

# ♅ URANUS

**Type:** Ice giant
**Distance from Sun:** ~2.87 billion km
**Diameter:** ~50,724 km
**Year:** ~84 Earth years
**Day:** ~17.2 hours
**Moons:** 27 known
**Atmosphere:** Hydrogen, helium, methane

### Description

Uranus is an **ice giant**.

Unlike Jupiter and Saturn, its interior contains much larger proportions of substances such as water, ammonia, and methane in high-pressure states.

Methane in its upper atmosphere absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight, giving Uranus its blue-green appearance.

### Extreme axial tilt

Uranus is tilted approximately **98 degrees**.

This means the planet essentially rotates on its side.

As a result, Uranus experiences extreme seasonal changes.

One pole can face the Sun for decades before the planet's orientation brings the opposite pole into sunlight.

### Rings

Uranus has a faint ring system.

Its rings are much darker and less spectacular than Saturn's.

### Interesting facts

* Uranus was the first planet discovered using a telescope.
* It rotates almost sideways.
* Its blue-green color comes largely from atmospheric methane.
* It has a complex magnetic field that is strongly tilted.

# ♆ NEPTUNE

**Type:** Ice giant
**Distance from Sun:** ~4.50 billion km
**Diameter:** ~49,244 km
**Year:** ~164.8 Earth years
**Day:** ~16.1 hours
**Moons:** 14 known
**Atmosphere:** Hydrogen, helium, methane

### Description

Neptune is the **farthest recognized planet from the Sun**.

It is an extremely cold and distant world with some of the fastest winds observed anywhere in the Solar System.

Like Uranus, Neptune is an ice giant.

### Atmosphere

Neptune's atmosphere contains:

* hydrogen
* helium
* methane

Methane contributes to its blue appearance.

### Storms

Neptune experiences enormous atmospheric storms.

Its winds can reach speeds of roughly **2,000 km/h**, making them among the fastest planetary winds known.

### Moon: Triton

Neptune's largest moon is **Triton**.

Triton is particularly interesting because it orbits Neptune in the opposite direction to Neptune's rotation.

This strongly suggests that Triton may have been captured by Neptune rather than forming there.

### Interesting facts

* Neptune takes almost 165 Earth years to orbit the Sun.
* It has extremely powerful winds.
* It was predicted mathematically before it was directly observed.
* Triton has nitrogen geysers and an unusual retrograde orbit.

# 🕳️ BLACK HOLE

**Type:** Compact object formed from gravitational collapse
**Not part of the Solar System**

For your COSMOS simulation, the black hole should be presented as a **distant astronomical object**, not as something physically located inside our Solar System.

### What is a black hole?

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that beyond a boundary called the **event horizon**, nothing—not even light—can escape.

A black hole itself is not a glowing sphere.

The darkness comes from the fact that light cannot escape from the region inside the event horizon.

### Event horizon

The event horizon is the boundary beyond which escape is impossible.

It is not a physical surface.

If you crossed the event horizon of a sufficiently large black hole, you would not hit a solid wall.

You would simply pass beyond the point from which signals could return to the outside universe.

### Accretion disk

If a black hole is surrounded by gas and dust, that material can form an **accretion disk**.

Friction, compression, and gravitational effects can heat the material to enormous temperatures.

The disk can therefore emit intense radiation.

This is the bright structure surrounding the dark black-hole shadow in your simulation.

### Gravitational lensing

A black hole bends spacetime.

Light traveling near it follows curved paths.

This can make light from the far side of an accretion disk appear above and below the black hole.

That is why your simulation has the characteristic:

```text
          BRIGHT LENSED DISK
             __________
          __/          \__
        _/                \_
       |      BLACK         |
       |      SHADOW        |
        \_                _/
          \__          __/
             \________/

========== ACCRETION DISK ==========
```

### Photon ring

Extremely close to a black hole, light can orbit along unstable paths.

This creates a phenomenon associated with the **photon ring** and contributes to the characteristic bright structure surrounding the black-hole shadow.

### Important distinction

**Black-hole shadow ≠ event horizon.**

The shadow is an apparent dark region produced by the way light behaves around the black hole. It is larger than the event horizon.

### Interesting facts

* Black holes can have enormous masses.
* They can merge with other black holes.
* Merging black holes produce gravitational waves.
* Supermassive black holes exist at the centers of many galaxies.
* The Milky Way contains a supermassive black hole called **Sagittarius A*** at its center.
* A black hole does not automatically "suck in" everything around it; objects can orbit a black hole just as planets orbit the Sun if their trajectories are appropriate.
